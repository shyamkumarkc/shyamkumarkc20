Debenture in Nepal

Last Updated: September 9, 2026
Debenture is a type of debt security used by companies and other eligible issuers to raise funds from investors. Investors generally receive interest according to the terms of the issue and repayment of principal at maturity, subject to the conditions of the particular debenture.

Debentures are an important part of Nepal's capital market. They can provide investors with an alternative to ordinary shares and may be suitable for investors seeking relatively predictable interest income. However, every debenture carries risks, including issuer credit risk, interest-rate risk and liquidity risk.

What is a Debenture?

Definition: A debenture is a debt instrument under which an issuer borrows money from investors and promises to pay interest and repay the principal according to predetermined terms.

When an investor purchases a debenture, the investor is essentially lending money to the issuing institution. Unlike ordinary shares, debenture holders do not normally receive ownership rights merely because they own the debt security.

How Does a Debenture Work?

A typical debenture has several important terms, such as face value, coupon or interest rate, maturity period, interest payment frequency and redemption conditions.

Term Meaning
Face Value The nominal value of the debenture.
Interest / Coupon Rate The stated rate at which interest is payable according to the issue terms.
Maturity The date or period when the principal becomes repayable.
Issuer The institution that raises funds by issuing the debenture.
Redemption Repayment of the principal amount according to the issue terms.
Market Price The price at which a listed debenture may trade in the secondary market.

Types of Debentures

1. Secured Debenture

A secured debenture is backed by specified security or collateral, subject to the terms of the issue. The security structure may provide additional protection to investors compared with an unsecured instrument, although it does not eliminate investment risk.

2. Unsecured Debenture

An unsecured debenture is not backed by specific collateral. Investors therefore depend more heavily on the issuer's ability to meet its contractual obligations.

3. Convertible Debenture

A convertible debenture contains terms that may allow conversion into equity securities under specified conditions.

4. Non-Convertible Debenture

A non-convertible debenture normally remains a debt instrument until redemption and does not provide a conversion feature into ordinary shares.

Key Features of Debentures

  • Debentures are debt instruments.
  • They generally have a specified face value.
  • They normally specify an interest or coupon rate.
  • Most debentures have a defined maturity period.
  • Interest payments are made according to the issue terms.
  • Listed debentures can potentially be traded in the secondary market.
  • Market price can differ from the face value.
  • Returns and risks depend on the specific issue and issuer.

Benefits of Investing in Debentures

Potential Benefits

  • Potentially predictable interest income.
  • Defined maturity period.
  • Can diversify an investment portfolio.
  • May be less volatile than some equity investments.
  • Listed debentures may provide secondary-market liquidity.

Risks

  • Issuer may fail to meet its obligations.
  • Market prices can fall.
  • Interest-rate changes can affect market value.
  • Some debentures may have limited liquidity.
  • Inflation can reduce the real value of fixed interest income.

How to Invest in Debentures in Nepal

The exact process depends on whether the debenture is being offered through a primary issue or purchased through the secondary market.

  1. Check the issue information: Review the issuer, interest rate, maturity, credit rating, security structure and other terms.
  2. Check eligibility: Confirm whether you are eligible to participate in the particular issue.
  3. Apply through the applicable channel: For eligible public issues, investors generally use the applicable securities application process.
  4. Receive the securities: Successful applicants receive the allotted securities in their dematerialized account according to the applicable process.
  5. Hold or trade: Investors may hold the debenture until maturity or, where the security is listed and tradable, sell it through the secondary market.

Important Factors to Check Before Buying a Debenture

  • Interest rate: Compare the coupon rate with available alternatives.
  • Maturity: Understand how long your money will remain invested.
  • Credit rating: Review the available credit rating and issuer's financial strength.
  • Issuer: Understand the financial position and business profile of the issuer.
  • Security: Check whether the debenture is secured or unsecured and understand the security structure.
  • Liquidity: Check whether there is an active secondary market.
  • Market price: If buying from the secondary market, compare the market price with face value and potential yield.
  • Issue terms: Read the official prospectus and applicable terms before investing.
Important: A high interest rate does not automatically mean a debenture is a better investment. Investors should evaluate credit risk, maturity, liquidity, market price and the financial condition of the issuer before making an investment decision.

Is Debenture a Good Investment?

Whether a debenture is suitable depends on an investor's financial objectives, risk tolerance, investment horizon and need for regular income.

Investors who prefer contractual interest payments and a defined maturity may consider debentures as part of a diversified portfolio. However, investors should not assume that a debenture is completely risk-free simply because it pays a fixed rate of interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a debenture?

A debenture is a debt security through which an issuer raises funds from investors and agrees to pay interest and repay principal according to specified terms.

What is debenture in Nepal?

A debenture in Nepal is a debt security issued by an eligible institution under applicable capital-market rules and issue terms.

Do debentures pay interest?

Most interest-bearing debentures provide interest according to their stated terms. The payment rate and frequency depend on the specific issue.

Can debentures be sold before maturity?

A listed and tradable debenture may generally be sold in the secondary market before maturity, subject to market availability and applicable trading rules.

Are debentures risk-free?

No. Debentures can carry issuer default risk, interest-rate risk, liquidity risk and other risks.

What should I check before investing in a debenture?

Investors should review the interest rate, maturity, issuer, credit rating, security, liquidity, market price and official issue documents.

Conclusion

Debentures are an important debt-investment instrument in Nepal's capital market. They can provide investors with interest income and a defined maturity structure while helping issuers raise long-term funds.

Before investing, investors should carefully study the official issue documents, credit rating, issuer's financial position, maturity, interest rate and liquidity. A debenture should be selected based on the investor's own objectives and risk tolerance rather than interest rate alone.
