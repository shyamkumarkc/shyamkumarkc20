Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON)

Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON) was established by the Government of Nepal on June 7, 1993 as the apex regulator of the securities market. It regulates Nepal's securities market under the Securities Act, 2006.

SEBON is responsible for regulating and developing the securities market, protecting investors and promoting transparency and orderly development of Nepal's capital market.

Departments of SEBON

SEBON has four major departments responsible for regulation, supervision, commodity market and research, and management and legal affairs.

  • Regulation Department
  • Supervision Department
  • Commodity Market and Research Department
  • Management and Legal Department

Major Functions and Duties of SEBON

  • Provide advice to the Government on matters related to the development of the capital market.
  • Issue necessary securities regulations and directives.
  • Register securities issued by public companies.
  • Regulate and systematize the issue, transfer, sale and exchange of registered securities.
  • Issue licenses to operate stock exchanges.
  • Issue licenses to stock brokers, dealers, merchant bankers and fund managers.
  • Issue licenses to depository companies, depository participants and credit rating agencies.
  • Register mutual funds and supervise collective investment schemes.
  • Approve the bylaws of stock exchanges and depository companies.
  • Take necessary actions to prevent insider trading and other securities-related offences.
  • Coordinate with relevant agencies to supervise and regulate the securities market.
  • Perform other functions necessary for the development of Nepal's capital market.

Importance of SEBON in Nepal's Capital Market

SEBON plays an important role in maintaining an orderly, transparent and regulated securities market in Nepal. Its regulatory activities cover securities issuers, stock exchanges, brokers, merchant bankers, mutual funds and other capital-market intermediaries.
