Filing and Its Objectives: Meaning, Importance, Types and Qualities

Filing is an important function of an office. It involves arranging, storing and preserving documents and records so that they can be located and used whenever required. A proper filing system helps an organization maintain past information for future reference, planning and decision-making.

What is filing?

Filing is the systematic process of classifying, arranging, storing and preserving documents and records for easy retrieval and future reference.

What is Filing?

Filing is an important function performed by offices and organizations. It involves storing papers, data, documents and records in an organized manner for future reference.

A proper filing system allows an office to preserve past information and retrieve it when required by planners, decision-makers and other authorized users.

“Filing is a process of arranging and storing records so that they can be located when required.”
— J. C. Denyer
In simple terms: Filing is a systematic process of classifying, storing and preserving documents and records so that they can be located quickly when required.

Objectives of Filing

The major objectives of filing are to protect records, provide evidence, save time, support planning and help in quick decision-making.

1. Protection of Documents

Past records can be used for future reference and may also serve as evidence when required. A proper filing system helps protect documents and records from water, heat, fire, insects, theft and other risks.

2. Providing Evidence in Case of Disputes

When records are properly preserved, relevant documents can be produced as evidence if misunderstandings or disputes arise.

3. Saving Time and Increasing Efficiency

A proper filing system makes documents and records easier to locate. This reduces the time required to search for information and improves office efficiency.

4. Providing Data for Formulating Plans and Policies

Past records provide useful information for planning and policy formulation. A good filing system helps organizations retrieve the historical information required for this purpose.

5. Supporting Quick Decision-Making and Prompt Action

A proper filing system enables decision-makers to obtain relevant information from past records. This information can support timely decisions and prompt action.

Importance of Filing

Filing is important because it helps an office systematically preserve records for future reference. Properly maintained records can be useful for decision-making, preparing budgets, formulating plans and policies, and carrying out other administrative activities.

Qualities of a Good Filing System

A good filing system should be simple, flexible, economical, safe, compact, accessible and suitable for the needs and growth of the organization.

1. Simplicity

A filing system should be simple and easy to understand so that employees can operate it without requiring specialized skills.

2. Flexibility or Elasticity

A filing system should be flexible enough to accommodate changes in the volume of transactions and records. It should allow the organization to increase or decrease its filing capacity when necessary.

3. Economy

A good filing system should be economical and should not create unnecessary expenses for the organization. Filing equipment and devices should be selected according to the organization's needs and resources.

4. Safety

A filing system should keep files and records safe and protect them from risks such as fire, theft, water, insects, rats and dust.

5. Compactness

Compactness means using the available space efficiently. A good filing system should occupy as little space as reasonably possible.

6. Accessibility

The filing system should allow documents to be filed and retrieved easily without unnecessary effort or cost.

7. Suitability

A filing system should be suitable for the size, capacity and growth of the organization. It should be capable of being adjusted according to changing requirements.

Types of Filing Systems

Filing systems can be organized using different methods depending on the nature, volume and requirements of an organization.

Traditional Filing System

Traditional filing systems use physical methods and devices for storing paper documents. Some traditional methods include spike or wire filing, pigeon or pocket filing, cardboard filing and pocket or baggage filing.

1. Spike or Wire Filing

Under this filing system, a wire or spike is used to keep temporary documents. Documents are inserted onto the wire, generally according to chronological order.

Advantages
  • Simple to understand and easy to operate.
  • Does not require special skills.
  • Economical because it requires only a wire or spike.
  • Requires relatively little space.
  • Takes little time to file a document.
Disadvantages
  • Not suitable for large volumes of records.
  • It may be inconvenient to retrieve a document located at the bottom.
  • Documents may tear while being inserted onto the wire.
  • Open storage may provide limited confidentiality.

2. Pigeon or Pocket Filing

In this system, an almirah or cabinet containing several small compartments, resembling pigeon holes, is used to keep documents. Documents may be arranged alphabetically.

Advantages
  • Simple to understand and easy to operate.
  • Does not require special skills.
  • Economical compared with more complex filing systems.
  • Documents can be located according to their alphabetical arrangement.
  • Provides some protection from dust and other environmental factors.
Disadvantages
  • May be unsuitable for large offices with high volumes of records.
  • Individual compartments have limited space.
  • Documents may be misplaced.
  • Filing and retrieval can become time-consuming when records increase.
  • Open compartments provide limited confidentiality.

3. Cardboard Filing

In cardboard filing, cardboard with paper flaps and laces is used to store documents. Documents may be arranged in chronological order and the cardboard is secured with laces.

Advantages
  • Simple to understand and easy to operate.
  • Economical because cardboard is inexpensive.
  • Requires relatively little space.
  • Requires little time for filing.
  • Provides physical protection to stored documents.
Disadvantages
  • May not be suitable for large offices.
  • Locating a particular document may be inconvenient.
  • Has limited capacity for storing documents.
  • Documents may be misplaced.

4. Pocket or Baggage Filing

In this system, documents belonging to a specified period are kept together in cloth or similar material and wrapped for storage. It is an older traditional method of physical filing.

Advantages
  • Simple to understand and easy to operate.
  • Requires few special skills.
  • Economical because simple materials can be used.
  • There may be less chance of individual documents being misplaced when stored together.
  • Requires relatively little space.
Disadvantages
  • Does not provide a sophisticated classification and indexing system.
  • Locating a specific document can be difficult.
  • Documents may be vulnerable to insects, rats, dust, water and fire if adequate protection is not provided.
